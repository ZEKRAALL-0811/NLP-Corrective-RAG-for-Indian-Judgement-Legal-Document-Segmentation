[86] Reasoning (35.8%) The Settlement Officer is not a Court and the provisions of the Civil Procedure Code are not applicable to the proceedings under the Act before the Settlement Officer.
[87] Reasoning (34.7%) Further, neither  under the Gudalur Janmam Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act 24/1969 nor the Rules framed thereunder, the Settlement Officer has any power to appoint any Commissioner to conduct local investigation of S.Nos.13,179,187,188,192 of Gudalur Village.
[88] Reasoning (31.3%) The Sangam comes into picture only as objectors after the notice in Form 6 was published by the Settlement Officer calling for objection, if any, for the grant of ryotwari patta to the owners of Silver Cloud Estate and the notice was published as per Rule 8(5) of the Janmam Rules.
[89] Reasoning (54.9%) The scope of any objection filed before the Settlement Officer in Form-6 notice is very limited and the Settlement Officer has merely to enquire into those objections by giving necessary opportunity to the objectors to make their submissions by producing any proof in support of those objections.
[90] Reasoning (52.6%) In this case, the Settlement Officer has already given necessary opportunity to the objector to make their submissions and to produce the records/proof in support of their objections and it is only after completion of the said procedure, the Settlement Officer has heard arguments advanced by the learned Advocates appearing for the Claimants as well as the Special Government Pleader representing the State Government and its Officers.
[91] Reasoning (35.2%) It is only thereafter on 08.08.2017 the Settlement Officer had reserved for orders on the application of the co-owners of Silver Cloud Estates.
[92] Reasoning (73.3%) While that being the factual position, there is no question of the Sangam filing the above petition on 29.08.2017 for appointment of a Court Commissioner to conduct a local investigation by wrongly calling themselves as claimants.
[93] Reasoning (61.1%) If the Sangam was a claimant for the grant of ryotwari patta, it ought to have filed the necessary application either under Section 8 or S.9 or S.10 of the Act on or before 27.05.1975.
[94] Reasoning (40.4%) No such application has been filed by the Sangam or any of its members.
[95] Reasoning (30.0%) In fact, the alleged Sangam was not in existence in the year 1975 and therefore could not have laid the claim at all.
[96] Reasoning (7.8%) The subject lands were inspected by the Settlement Officer along with the Tahsildar, Gudalur on 18.09.2017 as per the direction issued by the Hon'ble High Court in the order in W.P.No.12505/2013 dated 19.02.2014.
[97] Reasoning (13.8%) Rule 8(1) of the Janmam Estate (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Rules, 1974 defines that the application for ryotwari patta under Sections 8, 9 or 10 of the Act ought to have been submitted within six months from the appointed day, namely, six months from 27.11.1974.
[98] Reasoning (47.1%) Further, while claiming ryotwari patta in respect of the janman land, it has to be proved that such land was being cultivated by the claimant or their predecessors during the relevant period 1966-1969.
[99] Reasoning (52.8%) The Sangam or their members have not filed any evidence to prove that applications in Form-4 for issue of ryotwari patta for the subject land were filed within the period of limitation contemplated under Rule 8(1) of the Janmam Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Rules and there is no evidence on record to show that the subject lands claimed by the members of the Sangam were cultivated by them or by their family or predecessors during the relevant period 1966-1969 as required under Sections 8, 9 or 10 of the Act.

[291] None (12.0%) The present batch of appeals concerns the interpretation and application of a special agrarian reform legislation enacted to abolish the Janman Estates and regulate the grant of ryotwari pattas in accordance with the conditions prescribed by statute.

[293] None (47.7%) 27. Section 3 of the Gudalur Janmam Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act brought about the statutory vesting of every Janmam Estate in the Government with effect from the appointed day, namely, 27.11.1974, free from all encumbrances.
[294] None (35.4%) Consequently, any pre-existing proprietary or derivative right claimed by any Janmi, lessee, assignee or transferee stood extinguished except to the limited extent recognised under the Gudalur Janmam Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act itself.

[342] Reasoning (49.2%) The Tribunal has failed to reconcile these contemporaneous recitals while accepting the Claimant□s subsequent assertion that the entire estate had already been planted with tea, coffee and cardamom by 1960.
[343] Reasoning (30.2%) The Claimant has also failed to produce contemporaneous evidence demonstrating cultivation during the relevant statutory period such as revenue assessments, agricultural income-tax records, land revenue records and other contemporaneous public documents obtained from the concerned public authorities in support of its case.
[344] Reasoning (44.4%) Despite sufficient opportunity afforded before the Settlement Officer and the Tribunal, no contemporaneous documentary evidence establishing cultivation during the relevant three agricultural years was ever produced by the Claimant.
[345] Reasoning (87.4%) 35. Moreover, we could see that the Tribunal has only relied upon the averments contained in a counter affidavit filed by the Settlement Officer in separate proceedings, since the said counter affidavit was filed on 01.07.2013 decades after the relevant statutory period, which only states that the estate had developed the lands with plantation crops over the years.
[346] Reasoning (66.2%) In our considered opinion, such a statement cannot constitute proof that the lands were cultivated by the Claimant during the three agricultural years immediately preceding the 1st day of June, 1969, which alone is the enquiry mandated by Section 8.
[347] Reasoning (64.0%) It is pertinent to note that as per Section 8 of the Gudalur Janmam Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act, it is mandatory for the Janmi to have cultivated the lands for a continuous period of three agricultural years immediately before the 1st day of June, 1969.
[348] Reasoning (49.2%) As per Section 8, the cut-off date is 01.06.1969 and the Janmi should have cultivated the lands for a continuous period of  three years before the cut-off date.
[349] Reasoning (25.2%) The Claimant purchased the lands only in November 1966 and therefore it is evident that the Claimant does not satisfy the conditions required for obtaining the ryotwari patta.
[350] Reasoning (33.2%) Since the Gudalur Janmam Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act is an abolition statute, it extinguishes pre-existing Janmam rights upon vesting and thereafter creates a limited statutory entitlement to ryotwari patta in favour of persons who satisfy the conditions prescribed under Sections 8, 9 or 10.
[351] Reasoning (41.3%) Therefore, every Claimant seeking the benefit of Section 8 must necessarily succeed on the strength of compliance with the relevant provisions of the Act and the Rules made thereunder, but not merely on the strength of antecedent conveyances or assertions of ownership.
[352] Reasoning (84.5%) 36. We could further see that the Settlement Officer, after considering the entire record, has categorically held that no contemporaneous material established presentation of a valid application within the time prescribed under Rule 8.
[353] Reasoning (44.8%) When the burden of proving timely presentation rests entirely upon the Claimant, the Claimant has failed to discharge that burden by producing primary evidence.
[354] Reasoning (28.4%) However, the Tribunal accepted the Claimant□s assertion that an application dated 21.05.1975 had been filed, notwithstanding the absence of any acknowledgment, postal receipt, inward register, office endorsement or departmental record evidencing its receipt.
[355] Reasoning (34.6%) The Tribunal has proceeded on the premise that the long pendency of litigation before the Hon□ble Supreme Court justified a liberal approach to limitation.
[356] Reasoning (58.1%) This reasoning overlooks that the statutory period prescribed under Rule 8 expired in May, 1975, nearly two years before the interim orders passed by the Hon□ble Supreme Court.
[357] Reasoning (40.4%) The stay affected settlement proceedings, but it did not suspend the obligation to present an application within the statutory period.
[358] Reasoning (49.8%) Similarly, Rule 25 expressly prohibits admission of a belated application even upon proof of sufficient cause.
[359] Reasoning (31.4%) Ryotwari patta cannot be granted merely because the Claimant establishes a chain of private conveyances, as the Claimant was further required to establish timely presentation of its application, the legal character contemplated by Section 8 and continuous cultivation during the relevant statutory period.
[360] Reasoning (52.4%) When Section 8 requires proof that the lands were cultivated by the Janmi or through the modes recognised by the statute for a continuous period of three agricultural years immediately preceding the 1st day of June, 1969, the Tribunal failed to render clear findings regarding the evidence relied upon to establish cultivation during the relevant statutory period, the extent actually shown to have been cultivated, how the evidence corresponds to the 1705.90 acres for which patta has been directed or how the statutory requirement was satisfied in respect of each parcel of land.
[361] Reasoning (32.0%) Instead, cultivation appears to have been inferred from the Claimant□s general assertions and subsequent possession.
[362] Reasoning (38.2%) Such an inference cannot substitute for proof of the statutory requirement.
[363] Reasoning (50.8%) Even otherwise, it is not the claim of the Claimant that they filed the application under Section 17 as plantation lessees and their entire claim for grant of ryotwari patta is only on the ground that they filed the application under Section 8 within the limitation period, which claim has not been established through contemporaneous material.
[364] Decision (78.3%) For the aforementioned reasons, the impugned judgment suffers from errors of law, errors in appreciation of evidence and misapplication of the statutory provisions governing grant of ryotwari patta for an extent of 1705.90 acres in old Survey Nos.13, 179, 187, 188 & 192 of Gudalur Village and the same is liable to be set aside.
[365] Decision (99.0%) Accordingly, the judgment and decree dated  05.06.2020 passed by the Janmam Estates Abolition Tribunal and District Judge, The Nilgiris in C.M.A.No.1 of 2018 are set aside and S.T.A.No.25 of 2020 filed by the State stands allowed.
[366] Arguments of Petitioner (72.5%) 37. So far as the appeal filed by the Objector Sangam in S.T.A.No.19 of 2021 is concerned, they also assail the impugned judgment on the ground that within the subject lands claimed by the Claimant, is the lands inhabited by the Objector members, where houses, school, temples, burial grounds of all communities, cultivation lands etc., are situate and these villages are in Old S.Nos.13,179,187,188 and 192 of Gudalur Village and their families have traditionally been dependent on the forest for produce and sustenance and have been living in these lands since time immemorial, falling under the definition of ‚forest dwelling Scheduled Tribesƒ and other traditional forest dwellers of the Forest Rights Act, 2006.
[367] Reasoning (34.5%) However, the Tribunal has completely ignored these issues and allowed the claim of the Claimant for patta over these lands, without seeing that the lands under possession and enjoyment of the people living in these villages, are not under the possession or cultivation of the Claimant and hence no claim could be maintained in respect of these lands, as the Claimant did not fulfil either the requirements of Section 8 of the 1969 Act for grant of ryotwari patta or admittedly did not even possess rights to the said lands as a janmi for a continuous period of three agricultural years prior to 01.06.1969.
[368] Reasoning (73.0%) Equally, it is not even the claim of the members of the Objector Sangam that the subject lands have been cultivated by them during the relevant period 1966 to 1969 and that they had filed the application in Form No.4 within the stipulated time as per Rule 8(1) seeking for ryotwari patta and that they have filed the appeal only to the limited extent that their guaranteed rights under the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006 are sought to be defeated by the impugned judgment directing the grant of ryotwari patta to the Claimant.
[369] Decision (98.1%) Therefore, for the very same reasons and conclusions reached by us in the preceding paragraphs, the impugned judgment and decree are set aside and the S.T.A.No.19 of 2021 stands allowed with a direction to the official respondents to ensure that the dwelling units put up by the eligible forest dwellers/members of the Sangam are not disturbed from the declared forest area.
[370] Decision (39.8%) 38. So far as the appeal filed by the Claimant in S.T.A.No.40 of 2023 assailing the impugned order dated 15.07.2022 made in Unnumbered CFR No.2095 of 2021 in C.M.A.No.1 of 2018 is concerned, the Tribunal has declined the request of the Claimant for grant of ryotwari patta to an extent of 60.93 acres of land comprised in Old S.Nos.13A and 13/3A, R.S.Nos.750, 813/1 and 813/2 of Gudalur Village, for the reason that the said extent had already been notified as €Forest□ under Section 53 of the Gudalur Janmam Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act 24/1969 as per proceedings of the District Revenue Officer/Settlement Officer dated 11.08.2011.
[371] Reasoning (37.8%) The Claimant, in order to sustain the claim in respect of every parcel of land under Section 8, must independently satisfy the statutory requirements prescribed under the Gudalur Janmam Estates (Abolition and Conversion into Ryotwari) Act and cannot seek for ryotwari patta merely because adjoining lands are claimed or because larger extent of lands are the subject matter of connected proceedings.
[372] Reasoning (36.1%) The burden continued to rest upon the Claimant to establish that the said extent answered the statutory description under Section 8 and was not  otherwise excluded from grant of ryotwari patta.
[373] Reasoning (65.7%) As held by us earlier, the Claimant has failed to establish the compliance of the statutory requirements as mandated under Sections 8, 9 or 10 to sustain its claim.
[374] Reasoning (58.8%) Equally there is no quarrel with the ratio laid down in the judgments cited by the learned counsel for the Claimant, but the said judgments are not applicable to the facts of the present appeals.
[375] Reasoning (74.3%) In our considered opinion, the Tribunal, upon consideration of the relevant materials, has rightly declined the grant of ryotwari patta in respect of 60.93 acres of land notified as ‚Forestƒ to the Claimant.
[376] Reasoning (41.0%) The Claimant has not demonstrated any error of law, perversity in appreciation of evidence or jurisdictional infirmity in the impugned order and the appeal proceeds substantially on the assumption that once the Claimant establishes entitlement to ryotwari patta in respect of the remaining lands, the same consequence must necessarily follow in respect of the said 60.93 acres, which assumption is legally untenable.
[377] Decision (84.6%) Therefore, we find no grounds made out by the Claimant to interfere with the impugned order and accordingly, S.T.A.No.40 of 2023 filed by the Claimant stands dismissed.
[378] Decision (99.1%) 39. In the upshot, S.T.A.Nos.25 of 2020 & 19 of 2021 shall stand allowed and S.T.A.No.40 of 2023 shall stand dismissed with the above observations.
