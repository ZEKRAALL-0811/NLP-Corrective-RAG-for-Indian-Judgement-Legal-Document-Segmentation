# Literature Survey — Agentic Corrective RAG for Indian Legal Document Segmentation

| # | Title | Type | Authors / Inventors | Journal / Link | Year | Key Features | Limitations / Gaps | Differentiating Methodology |
|---|-------|------|----------------------|-----------------|------|---------------|----------------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | Pre-training Transformers on Indian Legal Text (InLegalBERT) | Paper | Shounak Paul, Arpan Mandal, Kripabandhu Ghosh, Saptarshi Ghosh | ACL Anthology / arXiv:2209.06049 | 2022 | BERT-base model continued-pretrained on 5.4M Indian court judgments (27GB, 1950–2019); benchmarked on statute identification, semantic segmentation, judgment prediction | Fixed 512-token input limit forces chunking; trained only on English-language SC/HC judgments, so coverage of vernacular or lower-court text is untested; segmentation task tested on small downstream sets | Continues MLM+NSP pretraining of LEGAL-BERT-SC on India-specific corpus rather than training from scratch, isolating the effect of domain-adaptive pretraining |
| 2 | Identification of Rhetorical Roles of Sentences in Indian Legal Judgments | Paper | Paheli Bhattacharya, Shounak Paul, Kripabandhu Ghosh, Saptarshi Ghosh, Adam Wyner | JURIX 2019 / arXiv:1911.05405 | 2019 | Defines the original 7-role rhetorical schema (Facts, RLC, Argument, Statute, Precedent, Ratio, RPC) for Supreme Court judgments; compares CRF vs. neural sequence models | Very small gold corpus (50 documents, 5 legal domains); labels show inter-annotator subjectivity, especially between Ratio/Precedent/Statute | First to move from handcrafted-feature CRFs to embedding-based neural sequence models (BiLSTM/BiLSTM-CRF) for this task |
| 3 | Corpus for Automatic Structuring of Legal Documents (BUILD / "Rhetorical Role" corpus) | Paper + Dataset | Prathamesh Kalamkar, Aman Tiwari, Astha Agarwal, Saurabh Karn, Smita Gupta, Vivek Raghavan, Ashutosh Modi | LREC 2022 / aclanthology.org/2022.lrec-1.470 | 2022 | 354 documents / ~40K sentences across SC, HC and District Courts, annotated with 13 fine-grained roles (Preamble, FAC, ISSUE, ARG_PETITIONER, ARG_RESPONDENT, ANALYSIS, STA, PRE_RELIED, RPC, etc.); baseline sequence-tagging models released | 13-way scheme increases class imbalance and boundary ambiguity vs. simpler 7-role schemes; no public test split, limiting reproducible benchmarking | Sentence-level annotation validated across three granularities (phrase/sentence/paragraph) before settling on sentence-level tagging; shows downstream gains on summarization and judgment prediction |
| 4 | LegalSeg: Unlocking the Structure of Indian Legal Judgments Through Rhetorical Role Classification | Paper + Dataset | Shubham Kumar Nigam, Tanmay Dubey, Govind Sharma, Noel Shallum, Kripabandhu Ghosh, Arnab Bhattacharya | NAACL Findings 2025 / arXiv:2502.05836 | 2025 | Largest rhetorical-role dataset to date — 7,120 documents, ~1.49M sentences, 7 roles; benchmarks BiLSTM-CRF, Transformer-over-InLegalBERT, GNNs, and an instruction-tuned RhetoricLLaMA (LLaMA-2-7B) | Even at this scale, closely related roles (e.g., Ratio vs. Precedent) remain hard to separate; instruction-tuned LLM (RhetoricLLaMA) underperforms structure-aware sequence models, showing LLMs alone are not yet sufficient | Directly compares graph-based, transformer-based, and generative-LLM approaches on one large corpus, establishing that sequential/contextual modeling still beats prompting-style LLM classification |
| 5 | Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks (RAG) | Paper | Patrick Lewis, Ethan Perez, Aleksandra Piktus, Fabio Petroni, Vladimir Karpukhin et al. | NeurIPS 2020 / arXiv:2005.11401 | 2020 | Introduces the base RAG architecture combining a parametric seq2seq generator (BART) with a differentiable dense retriever (DPR) over Wikipedia; RAG-Sequence and RAG-Token variants | Retrieval is trusted unconditionally — no mechanism to detect or correct irrelevant/noisy retrieved passages, so bad retrieval directly propagates into hallucinated answers | End-to-end differentiable training of retriever + generator, treating the retrieved document as a latent variable, rather than a static two-stage pipeline |
| 6 | Corrective Retrieval Augmented Generation (CRAG) | Paper | Shi-Qi Yan, Jia-Chen Gu, Yun Zhu, Zhen-Hua Ling | arXiv:2401.15884 | 2024 | Lightweight retrieval evaluator grades retrieved documents (Correct / Ambiguous / Incorrect) and triggers corrective actions; decompose-then-recompose strips retrieved text down to "knowledge strips"; falls back to web search when local corpus is insufficient | Relies on an external, often proprietary, large-scale web search API for the "Ambiguous"/"Incorrect" fallback, which is hard to reproduce and inappropriate for closed legal corpora; evaluator trained on general QA data, not legal text | Directly addresses RAG's blind trust in retrieval by inserting a self-correction/grading loop before generation, rather than assuming retrieved context is always usable |
| 7 | RAGAS: Automated Evaluation of Retrieval Augmented Generation | Paper + Framework | Shahul Es, Jithin James, Luis Espinosa-Anke, Steven Schockaert | EACL 2024 (System Demos) / arXiv:2309.15217 | 2023 | Reference-free evaluation suite defining Faithfulness, Answer Relevance, and Context Relevance ("RAG Triad") using an LLM-as-judge, validated against the WikiEval human-annotated dataset | Metrics depend on the quality/consistency of the underlying LLM judge; evaluated only on open-domain Wikipedia QA, not on domain-specific (e.g., legal) faithfulness where claim atomicity is harder | Introduces claim-decomposition-based, ground-truth-free scoring (no need for human reference answers), which DeepEval's FaithfulnessMetric operationalizes as a Pytest-native CI/CD check |
